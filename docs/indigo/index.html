Suite / Indigo

Indigo

A fork of Swiss for the Nintendo GameCube with the interface rebuilt.

An animated Home, a poster library and game details, drawn in the console's own visual language.

GPL-2.0-or-laterNintendo GameCubeSD card · drag and dropC · devkitPPC

Download Indigo 2.1.0Read the guideSource on GitHub

Indigo-v2.1.0.zip · 5.8 MB · Release notes · All downloads

Indigo is an unofficial fork of Swiss, the homebrew utility that boots and patches games on a Nintendo GameCube. The fork changes one thing: what you look at. Everything underneath — the device handlers, the patch engine, the loader — is upstream's work, and this fork does not try to improve it. It is for people who use Swiss daily on real hardware and want it to feel like it belongs on the console.

Features

Home is a glass cube

Four faces, one destination each, and a fifth, Apps, when your card has programs: left and right turn it, up and down tip it over. It flies in when Indigo starts, and it is clear glass: it bends what is behind it, parts the light at its edges and mirrors soft lights that slide across it as it turns. The Library face is a controller that mirrors yours.

Home

Your games as posters

A row of covers with two either side of the game in the middle, a column with the title beside each cover, or a grid five covers wide. Box art comes from one ready-made pack for every region, or your own; without one, each game shows its disc banner.

Library

Spotlight: the game, not just its box

A fourth layout: the selected game's gameplay still fills a 4:3 panel, with its title, publisher and a few sentences about it beside it, over a row of disc banners. The poster pack carries a still and a description for every game. Its design is mvizensk's Gameplay Spotlight, used with permission.

Spotlight

Your homebrew in Apps

Emulators, Game Boy Interface and other programs go in /apps and get a face of the cube to themselves, shown as posters the way the Library shows games. Each poster is a PNG you put beside the program, converted on the console; a program without one gets a poster of its name.

Apps

Game details, not a dialogue

Artwork, last played, cheats, the game's own settings and its boot options on one screen. Up and down move between Launch Game, Cheats and Settings, and A opens the one you're on.

Game details

A launch screen, not progress boxes

Starting a game centres its cover in a ring that fills as Indigo applies the game's settings and your cheats and loads it, one line saying which step it is on. Then the screen fades to the game.

Start the game

Cheats that read clearly

Gecko codes switch on and off one by one, with each cheat's state plain to see. Codes the files show would break Indigo are switched off and marked.

Cheats

Settings that read like the console

Quick settings on one screen, Defaults for every game, Setup in six sections, and each game's own settings marked Custom where they differ. A new video mode waits for A, and only stays if you confirm it.

Settings

Music with the console's sound

"Up in the Sky" by Memoraphile, given the GameCube's own 32 kHz sound: a quiet intro, then a section that loops without a seam. Soft sounds follow as you move and choose.

Music and sounds

Eight colors, sixteen icons

Menu Color recolors the whole interface in Indigo, Azure, Emerald, Gold, Spice, Crimson, Rose or Jet Black, and each face of the cube has four icons to choose from.

Make it yours

Every device by its picture

Change Source lifts the cube out of the way and lines up the devices Indigo found, each with its picture. Under the one in the middle it says what the device can do, where it plugs in and whether Indigo found it.

Source

Made for your TV

Menu Widescreen draws Indigo for a TV set to 16:9, keeping the cube, the text and every menu in shape, and the cube's edges stay smooth on a large or upscaled screen.

Every setting

Memory Cards

A save explorer under System: every save with its banner and comment, and Copy, Move and Delete between Slot A, Slot B and folders on your SD card. Every copy is read back before a Move removes the original.

Memory Cards

Reset back to Indigo

With In-Game Reset set to Apploader, A + Z + START during a game returns to Indigo. The download carries the file that makes it work.

Leaving a game

See it in action

Install: drag and drop

  1. Download

    Indigo-v2.1.0.zip. Everything inside it is laid out exactly as it goes on the card.

  2. Keep your Swiss, then drag

    First, if the card has an ipl.dol, that is your current Swiss: rename it to z.dol before copying anything. With PicoBoot or PicoLoader, holding Z at power-on starts it. Then unzip, select everything inside and drag it onto the root of the card. On a Mac, hold Option as you drop and choose Merge: Replace deletes what is already in the card's swiss folder, your settings, cheats and saves.

  3. Add games and power on

    Put your disc images in /games (make the folder if the card has none), one folder per game or the images directly, with nothing else in it or in the game folders. On Home, turn the cube to Library and press A.

Updating from Indigo 1.x? ipl.dol is already Indigo, so don't rename it: copy the new files over it, choosing Merge on a Mac. If 1.25.0 made you rename stock Swiss to swiss.dol, rename it back to z.dol: Indigo no longer starts it by itself (issue 3).

Your loaderWhat to do
PicoBoot, PicoLoader and modchips that boot /ipl.dolNothing more: Indigo is the ipl.dol the download puts on the card. Hold Z at power-on for the z.dol you kept.
PicoBoot or PicoLoader with Swiss in its flash, and no ipl.dol on the cardFirst flash the firmware that starts the card's ipl.dol: PicoBoot's picoboot_full_pico.uf2 (installation guide) or PicoLoader's picoloader_gekkoboot.uf2. Then drag and drop as above.
A loader that boots another .dol by nameCopy ipl.dol over that file, keeping its name: FlippyDrive, for one, boots boot.dol.
GC Loader, and loaders that boot a disc imageStart ipl.dol from Swiss's file browser.

Your Swiss settings carry over, and stock Swiss reads the same settings file; when it saves, it leaves out the settings only Indigo has, such as Menu Color. The install guide covers updating, going back to stock Swiss and building from source.

Posters and cheats: good to know

  • Unzip a pack and drag its swiss folder onto the root of the card; unzipping over existing files replaces cheat files of the same name. Posters: 1,622 games from every region, with 1,574 gameplay stills and a description for all 1,708 (about 91 MB). Cheats: 609 games, 22,960 cheats (about 1.4 MB).
  • One poster pack covers every region, so there is nothing to pick. It needs Indigo 2.1 or later; 2.0 and earlier read only the regional packs their release notes link. A game without a poster shows its disc banner, and in Spotlight a game without a still shows its cover.
  • Files named like GALE01_v102.txt are for one disc revision; Indigo picks the right one on its own. A few PAL games have one file per language: GAFP01.txt is the English one, and for another language you copy GAFP01_fre.txt (or _ger, _ita, _spa) over it.
  • Cheats are provided as published and are not play-tested; save first. 290 cheats that the files show would break Indigo are switched off and marked [disabled for Indigo]. That check is a filter for known ways a cheat breaks Indigo (a cut-off code block, an Action Replay line, a write into memory Indigo uses), not a guarantee. 2,860 cheats run their own code or write through a pointer the game sets while it runs; those can only be judged on the console, as with any loader.

Checksums: each Indigo release lists its SHA-256 on GitHub; the packs' are in SHA256SUMS.txt. Box art and descriptions from GameTDB; the artwork belongs to each game's publisher. Gameplay stills from libretro-thumbnails. The Spotlight layout's design is Gameplay Spotlight by mvizensk, used with permission. Cheats from the gc-forever WiiRD/Gecko code collection (07.04.2023) by Ralf and its contributors, and the geckocodes.org database via codes.rc24.xyz; authors are credited in each file. Not affiliated with Nintendo. Rights holder or problem? Open an issue.

Releases

Releases

Each vX.Y.Z on main is a release, marked Latest on GitHub: the one for everyday use.

Release candidates

Before a release ships, a release candidate, vX.Y.Z-rc.N, goes out as a GitHub pre-release for a last round of testing, and its release notes say how to try it. This page offers the release: take that one for everyday use.

Every build is traceable

Each zip is built from its tagged commit in a pinned container and published with its SHA-256 and a build provenance attestation, and System › System Information shows the commit it was built from.

Questions

Does Indigo replace Swiss?

It is Swiss, with a different interface. Everything Swiss does, from the device handlers to the patches and boot options, works the same way, and your settings carry over in both directions. Keep stock Swiss as z.dol if you want it one button away.

Which consoles and loaders does it run on?

Any GameCube that already runs Swiss and reads an SD card, in an SD2SP2, an SD Gecko or the loader's own slot; or a Wii with GameCube ports, running Swiss in GameCube mode with the card in an SD Gecko. PicoBoot and other modchips that boot ipl.dol start it directly, and disc-image loaders such as GC Loader start it from Swiss. See Install.

I only see Swiss's plain file list, not the Library.

The Library takes over only when /games holds disc images and nothing else. Any other file or an empty folder, in /games or in a game's folder, keeps Swiss's list. Troubleshooting covers this and the other common surprises.

Can I start emulators and other homebrew?

Yes. Put their .dol files in /apps, and Home gets an Apps face that shows them as posters and starts them the way Swiss's file list does, .cli and .dcp files included. Game Boy Interface's download is already laid out that way. Settings › Setup › Console › Apps Face hides the face if you don't want it. See Apps.

Where do the posters and cheats come from?

The poster pack is GameTDB's box art, cropped to the front cover, with gameplay stills from libretro-thumbnails and descriptions from GameTDB; the cheat pack is the gc-forever collection plus geckocodes. You can build a pack from your own covers and screenshots instead: see Posters.

Is it affiliated with Nintendo or with the Swiss project?

No. Indigo is an unofficial fork, not endorsed by or affiliated with the Swiss project or Nintendo. Please report Indigo problems to Indigo, never to upstream Swiss.
